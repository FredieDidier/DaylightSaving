In 2019 Brazil abolished daylight saving time, moving an hour of summer light from evening to morning for 131 million people. I compare the change after abolition in road deaths during the clock hour that turned dark in each municipality and day with the change in the same hour in states without daylight saving time. These deaths rose 34 percent; pedestrians, 12 percent of them before, account for 43 percent of the increase. The morning hour that gained light shows no offsetting decline. Abolition cost 54 lives per summer, a lower bound. Mexico's 2022 abolition shows the same pattern.
