In 2019 Brazil abolished daylight saving time, moving an hour of summer light from evening to morning for 131 million people. We compare road deaths in the clock hour that turned dark in each municipality and day with the same hour in states that kept standard time. These deaths rose 34 percent, and pedestrians account for 43 percent of the increase. The effect follows the local sun, not the clock, and the brighter mornings showed no detectable decline in deaths. If hours whose light did not change were unaffected, abolition added 62 crash-scene deaths each summer, mid-November to mid-February.
