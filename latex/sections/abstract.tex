In 2019 Brazil abolished daylight saving time, moving an hour of summer light from evening to morning for 131 million people. I compare road deaths in the clock hour that turned dark in each municipality and day with the same hour in states without it. These deaths rose 34 percent, and pedestrians account for 43 percent of the increase. The effect follows the local sun, not the clock, and the brighter mornings did not offset it: abolition added 74 deaths at the scene per DST season. Deaths in the same hour also rose after Mexico's 2022 abolition.
